# Historical Data Audit

Generated by `python -m fantasyprep.historical.dataset.build`. Every number
below is computed from the raw file at build time, not transcribed by hand --
re-run the build to refresh it.

## Source

- **Path**: `data/historical/raw/combined_nfl_seasons_1999_2024.csv` (immutable -- never written to)
- **Rows**: 33,636 across 52 columns
- **Upstream**: nflverse seasonal player stats. This is the *same source* the live
  pipeline already pulls via `nfl_data_py.import_seasonal_data`
  (`historical/sources/nfl_stats.py`). Its value-add is the extra 1999-2009
  seasons, and being frozen -- live pulls drift as nflverse revises data.

## Two traps in the raw file

Both would corrupt anything built on this data, and neither is visible after
the fact. They're the reason `loader.py` exists rather than a bare `read_csv`.

### 1. `season_type` triple-counts every player-season

The file holds three *views of the same seasons*, not three sets of players:

| season_type | rows |
|---|---|
| `POST` | 3,362 |
| `REG` | 15,102 |
| `REG+POST` | 15,172 |

`REG+POST` overlaps `REG` on 15,102 player-seasons and reports **different** fantasy points for **3,105** of them.
Reading the file unfiltered inflates season totals with playoff production.
We keep `REG` only: **15,102 rows**, matching the existing
pipeline's `s_type="REG"`.

### 2. Air-yards columns are fabricated zeros before 2006, not nulls

nflfastR's air-yards charting begins in 2006. Before that, these columns are *zero*, so they pass a null check while containing no information. A model trained across the full history would learn that nobody had air yards in 2003.

| column | pre-2006 null | pre-2006 nonzero | post-2006 nonzero | pre-2006 max | post-2006 max |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `passing_air_yards` | 0% | 0.0% | 17.3% | 0.0 | 6481.0 |
| `passing_yards_after_catch` | 0% | 0.0% | 14.8% | 0.0 | 2830.0 |
| `pacr` | 82% | 0.0% | 15.1% | 0.0 | 11.5 |
| `receiving_air_yards` | 0% | 0.0% | 83.5% | 0.0 | 2753.0 |
| `receiving_yards_after_catch` | 0% | 3.7% | 79.8% | 185.0 | 987.0 |
| `racr` | 21% | 0.0% | 79.3% | 0.0 | 507.0 |
| `air_yards_share` | 100% | 0.0% | 83.5% | None | 0.54 |
| `wopr` | 100% | 0.0% | 84.0% | None | 0.95 |

`receiving_yards_after_catch` is the subtlest case: it isn't uniformly zero
pre-2006, it's *fragmentary* -- a handful of players per season with a season
maximum an order of magnitude below the 2006 figure. That's partial charting,
not real production, and it's more dangerous than an honest zero because it
looks alive. `loader.mask_uncollected_eras` converts all of these to NaN.

## Identity

- `player_id` is the nflverse GSIS id (format `00-0000003`) -- the same id space as `import_weekly_data`, `import_players`, and `import_ids`, so future joins to weekly data and player metadata need **no fuzzy name matching**.
- **Stable across seasons**: 2,799 players appear in more than one season, up to 23 seasons for a single id.
- **Duplicate (player_id, season) rows after the REG filter: 0.** One row per player-season, confirmed rather than assumed. `loader.load_regular_season` raises if this ever stops being true instead of silently de-duplicating.
- **Name columns**: `player_name` is 52% null and unusable; `player_display_name` is 0% null and is the real name column. The loader substitutes it.
- **1 orphan row(s) have no name *and* no position** (e.g. `00-0005532`, 1999, NO, 3 games) and are dropped by the loader -- unjoinable to ADP and unrankable. This is reported rather than silent, and the loader still fails outright if unnamed rows ever exceed 0.1% of the file, so a systemic name-resolution failure can't slip through as a rounding error. Canonical row count is therefore 15,101, not 15,102.
- **Team changes do not create duplicate rows** (max distinct `recent_team` per player-season: 1). A traded player has one row carrying his *last* team. **Limitation**: team-split season lines are not recoverable from this file, so any future team-context feature (offensive environment, pace, target competition) needs the weekly data instead.

## Fantasy-point validation

The source's `fantasy_points_ppr` column was **not** assumed to mean what its name suggests. We reproduced it with our own scoring function at candidate passing-TD values across all 13,415 regular-season skill-position rows:

| our scoring variant | max abs delta | mean abs delta | rows off by >0.5 | exact match |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `pass_td_4` | 0.0 | 0.0 | 0 | **yes** |
| `pass_td_6` | 110.0 | 2.8686 | 1,634 | no |

**Conclusion: the source column is full PPR with 4-point passing touchdowns.** Our league uses 6. The reproduction at `pass_td=4.0` is exact to 0.00 across every row, which is a strong independent validation of `nfl_stats.compute_points` itself -- the same function the live pipeline and the backtest score with.

Under our real settings the largest gaps are all quarterbacks, exactly as expected:

| player | season | pos | pass TDs | ours (6pt TD) | source (4pt TD) | delta |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Peyton Manning | 2013 | QB | 55 | 519.98 | 409.98 | +110.0 |
| Tom Brady | 2007 | QB | 50 | 490.04 | 390.04 | +100.0 |
| Patrick Mahomes | 2018 | QB | 50 | 517.08 | 417.08 | +100.0 |

Each delta is exactly `2 x passing_tds`. Across all rows our scoring differs from the source's by a mean of 2.8686 points, with 1,413 rows differing by more than 2.

**Therefore `fantasy_points_ppr` is kept only as `fantasy_points_nflverse_ppr`, a cross-check column. It is never the outcome variable.** `fantasy_points` in the canonical table is computed under this league's real settings.

### `data/historical/player_season_features.parquet`

14,045 rows x 94 columns -- QB/RB/WR/TE only, with ranks, per-game rates, efficiency ratios, and prior-season lags.

**Ranking methodology.** Ranks are computed on `fantasy_points` under our
scoring, never the source column. Ties use `min` (two players tied for 5th are
both 5, next is 7). The pool is every player at that fantasy position who
recorded a regular season, not just drafted ones -- restricting to drafted
players would bias the replacement-level tail. PPG rank comes in two variants:
`position_rank_ppg` ranks everyone (a 2-game cameo can top it), and
`position_rank_ppg_qualified` ranks only players with >= 8
games, leaving the rest NaN. Both are kept because which is correct depends on
the question.

## Leakage safety

The eventual point of this data is leakage-safe held-out backtests, so the pre-season / outcome split is enforced in code rather than by convention (`features.py`):

- **`PRE_SEASON_COLUMNS`** (28 present here): identity, plus `prev_*` values and `seasons_of_history`. Legal model inputs for predicting that season.
- **Outcome columns** (66): everything else -- that season's own stats, rates, ranks and points. Target variables only.
- The outcome set is defined as the *complement* of the pre-season set, not as a second hand-maintained list, so a newly added column is treated as an outcome until someone deliberately classifies it. It fails closed.
- `preseason_frame(df, season)` is the sanctioned way to build model inputs.

Two classifications worth calling out as deliberate:

- **`recent_team` is an outcome.** A team is usually known in August, but this column holds the player's *last* team, so for anyone traded midseason it encodes information that didn't exist at draft time. `prev_recent_team` is the safe version.
- **`yoy_fantasy_change` is an outcome**, despite reading like a feature: season Y's change is (Y minus Y-1) and therefore contains Y. The usable form is `prev_yoy_fantasy_change`.

Prior-season lags only ever carry a *strictly adjacent* season forward. A player who missed a full season gets NaN for his `prev_*` values rather than a two-year-old season quietly relabelled as "last year"; `seasons_of_history` preserves the fact that he has a past.

## Outcome bucket study

Full numbers in `data/historical/outcome_bucket_study.json`. This measures whether `historical/outcomes.py`'s `BUCKET_WIDTH = 3` is justified. **No simulator parameter was changed** -- that needs its own A/B backtest.

- **(a) ADP-rank buckets**: seasons 2010-2024. Directly comparable to today's buckets. Limited to 2010+ because that is FFC ADP's coverage floor -- **the 1999-2009 seasons in this dataset have no market rank at all**, which is the single biggest gap and the subject of `HISTORICAL_ADP_RESEARCH.md`.
- **(b) Prior-finish-rank buckets**: seasons 2000-2024, all 26 years. Leakage-safe by construction and usable across the full history.

## Known limitations

1. **No market rank before 2010.** The dataset's 1999-2009 seasons cannot be
   bucketed by ADP at all. This is the binding constraint on using the extra
   history for anything ADP-conditioned.
2. **No age, birth date, draft position, or rookie year.** Not in this file at
   all -- see `PLAYER_METADATA_RESEARCH.md`.
3. **No team-split lines for traded players.** One row per player-season with
   the last team only.
4. **No snap counts or route counts**, so opportunity is limited to targets and
   carries; true usage rate is not derivable here.
5. **No weekly granularity**, so floor/ceiling/volatility/consistency cannot be
   computed from this file -- see `WEEKLY_DATA_RESEARCH.md`.
6. **Pre-2006 is a different feature regime**, not merely sparser data.
7. **No DST or kicker scoring**, the same documented gap as the rest of the
   codebase (`nfl_stats.POSITION_MAP`), not something new here.
8. **2025 is absent** -- the frozen file ends at 2024. `LivePlayerSeasonSource`
   exists for when nflverse publishes it.

### Uncertain / unverified

Stated explicitly rather than guessed:

- **`games`** is assumed to be games *played* (not active/inactive). Range observed is 1-17, consistent with that reading and with the 17-game era, but not independently confirmed against a box-score source.
- **`dakota`** and **`pacr`/`racr`** are nflfastR composite metrics whose exact formulas we have not re-derived. They are passed through unmodified and should not be relied on until verified.
- **`sacks`** is stored as an integer here, though half-sacks exist in the official record. It does not affect fantasy scoring under our settings.
- **`special_teams_tds`** is credited at 6 points by our scoring, the standard convention, but leagues vary and this was not verified against our platform's actual settings.
- **Pre-2006 `target_share`/`receiving_epa` are present and non-null**, so play-by-play-derived data does extend back to 1999 for those; only the air-yards family starts in 2006. We did not verify the pre-2006 play-by-play charting is of equal quality to the modern era.

## Recommended next steps

1. **Historical ADP is the binding constraint.** See
   `HISTORICAL_ADP_RESEARCH.md`. Without market rank before 2010, 11 of the 26
   seasons cannot be used for anything ADP-conditioned.
2. **Player metadata (age) is the highest-value missing feature** for the
   eventual comparable-player model. See `PLAYER_METADATA_RESEARCH.md`.
3. **Weekly data** unlocks floor/ceiling/volatility. Partly already built --
   see `WEEKLY_DATA_RESEARCH.md`.
4. **Do not change `BUCKET_WIDTH`** on the strength of this study alone; it
   needs an A/B backtest like every other simulator change.
